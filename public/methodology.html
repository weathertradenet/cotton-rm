How the explorer is built

The explorer harmonizes annual subnational cotton statistics from eleven countries into planted area in hectares and yield in tonnes per hectare. Open any question below; opening a new answer automatically closes the previous one.

1. Yield forecasting using weather anomalies

Environmental conditions during the growing season can explain approximately 60% of crop failures. The remaining 40% may be attributed to poor management practices and political and economic factors, including the availability and cost of fertilisers and fuel.

2. Data-driven yield modeling
Weather anomalies considered for cotton-yield modeling:
  • Frost days: Tmin < 0°C
  • Dry days: Precipitation < 1 mm/day
  • Rainy days: Precipitation ≥ 1 mm/day
  • Heavy-rain days: Precipitation ≥ 10 mm/day
  • Extremely hot days: Tmax > 40°C
  • Hot nights: Tmin > 25°C
  • Insufficient-heat days: Tmean < 15.6°C
  • Windy days: Daily maximum 10 m wind ≥ 30 mph
  • High-moisture days: Daily mean relative humidity ≥ 80%
  • Insufficient-sunlight days: Incoming solar radiation < 60% of clear-sky radiation

For larger agricultural areas, large infrastructure projects, and disruption risk analysis, the climate risk data can be provided for larger areas at 1–30 kilometre resolution.

4. Cotton yield statistics: is it Open Data?
Cotton data for the US is created and provided by the United States Department of Agriculture National Agricultural Statistics Service, and can be downloaded here. For Brazil the source is the IBGE’s Municipal Agricultural Production dataset. Mexico: SIAP.
5. Which geographic level is the best for crop modeling?
The best resolution is county and municipality level.
6. How should I read the weather-anomaly heatmap?

The historical reference is 1991–2025. Pale cells represent normal conditions within ±1 standard deviation; red represents above-normal anomalies and blue represents below-normal anomalies. Future five-year projection windows appear at the right.

Select “Overall growing season” for the complete cotton season, or use the growth-period selector to inspect a specific development phase. Select the question-mark tooltip beside an indicator to see its technical definition. Future columns use bias-corrected CMIP6 climate data supplied through Open-Meteo.
